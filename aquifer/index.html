Research behind the game

This game is a simplified scenario, not a measured model of any real basin. The relationships below come from published research led at UC Santa Barbara's Bren School; the numbers in the game (well depths, pumping rates, costs, rainfall) are invented so the story plays out in 20 turns.

Research-backed relationships

  • Pumping lowers the water table most steeply around the well that pumps, in a cone of depression that spreads to neighbours.
  • Wells fail long before an aquifer is empty. Shallower wells, often household wells, run dry first when water levels drop by only a few metres.
  • Groundwater declines are widespread and, in many aquifers, accelerating. Recoveries exist, and they follow deliberate interventions: alternative supplies, pumping rules, managed recharge.
  • Rivers and aquifers trade water. When the water table drops below the river bed, a river that once gained water from the ground starts losing water into it, and can disconnect.
  • Managed recharge depends on where you put it: geology, rainfall intensity and land use govern how much water actually reaches the aquifer.
